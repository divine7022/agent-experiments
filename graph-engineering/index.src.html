Graph engineering my agent setup

My pipeline was mostly a chain: eight stages, one small fan-out, and loops that stopped "when clean". I audited it as a graph and changed its shape.

The idea is from Austin Marchese's video, Stanford's Graph Method Explained, which builds on a course by Andrew Ng. Every agent workflow is a graph of steps. Four shapes cover most of them, and each one fails in its own way. The figures here are redrawn for this page; the ideas in the first eight are his.

The four shapes

The wait test

One question, asked of every arrow in the graph:

Does this step actually need the result of the one before it?

If the answer is no, the two steps are not a chain. They can run together.

Where each shape breaks

My pipeline, before

The audit found three things. The planner waited for research it did not need. Two reviewers read the same artifact one after the other. And the fix loop had no number on it.

My pipeline, after

Every fan-out brief now carries a line saying what the other workers cover, and every merge says who reported, so an overlap or a missing worker is visible.
